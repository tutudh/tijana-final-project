\begin{center}
{\Large\bfseries Online PAC Labeling with Joint Error and Cost Guarantees\par}
\medskip
Selective expert feedback, constrained routing, and a matching cost exponent
\end{center}

\begin{abstract}
We study online labeling with a cheap predictor and a costly, correct
expert. Each expert query both prevents a released-label error and reveals
feedback about the predictor; released model labels supply no feedback.
The objective is to control cumulative released error while approaching
the cost of an optimal feasible score-based policy. Under i.i.d. arrivals,
a fixed predictor and score, and a known global Lipschitz bound on
conditional model error, we propose one-sided barrier saddle routing
(OBSR). It learns a possibly nonmonotone release policy and an error
price from selectively revealed labels. We prove simultaneous
high-probability bounds of order $\widetilde O(T^{2/3})$ on cumulative
error excess and cost regret against all feasible Borel score policies.
An explicit target-tightening condition gives nominal PAC error control
with sublinear excess cost. A complementary information-theoretic
construction yields a $T^{2/3}$ lower bound on a weighted cost--error
tradeoff. Together, these results determine the minimax cost exponent,
up to logarithmic factors, for a specified nominal-PAC class with failure
probability $T^{-2}$. We provide explicit updates and complete proofs;
empirical validation is left to future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{From error control to cost-efficient labeling}
\label{sec:intro}

Large-scale labeling combines inexpensive model predictions with costly
expert judgments. A useful guarantee must address both the accuracy of
the released labels and the cost of producing them. Sending every item
to a correct expert trivially controls error; achieving that control
while learning when the model can safely be used is the statistical
problem. We ask whether an online learner can approach the cost of an
optimal error-constrained policy when true labels are available only
through paid expert queries.

\paragraph{The optimization principle.}
Let $S$ be an observed score and $m(s)$ the model's conditional error
probability. A score policy releases the model label with probability
$a(s)$ and otherwise uses the expert. With unit expert cost, its
expected expert expenditure and released error are
$\E[1-a(S)]$ and $\E[a(S)m(S)]$. The population oracle solves
\begin{equation}
 \inf_{a:[0,1]\to[0,1]}\E[1-a(S)]
 \quad\text{subject to}\quad
 \E[a(S)m(S)]\le\eps,
 \label{eq:oracle-intro}
\end{equation}
where policies are Borel measurable. At a fixed error price
$\lambda\ge0$, the Lagrangian integrand is
$1+a(s)(\lambda m(s)-1)$. It favors release when $\lambda m(s)<1$
and deferral when $\lambda m(s)>1$, with randomization possible at
equality. This linear-programming interpretation explains the role of
the price; it is a reformulation, not a novelty claim. The learner must
estimate useful routing decisions without knowing $m$ or receiving
unpaid labels. Moreover, a threshold in $m(s)$ need not be a threshold
in $s$: the score need not be calibrated or monotone in actual error.

\paragraph{Method and results.}
OBSR learns release probabilities on score bins together with a dual
error price. Its one-sided log barrier accommodates the
inverse-probability feedback supplied by expert deferrals. A vanishing
query floor maintains information while limiting exploration cost.
The main results are a simultaneous finite-horizon error and cost
guarantee (Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}), a sufficient condition for nominal
error control (Corollary~\ref{cor:tightening}), and a lower bound for
every adaptive learner in a specialized instance family
(Theorem~\ref{thm:main}). Corollary~\ref{cor:minimax} connects the upper
and lower results under a precisely defined nominal-PAC criterion.
The comparator knows the population law but cannot inspect the current
true label before deciding. All paid model and expert calls are counted.

\paragraph{Relation to existing methods.}
PAC labeling provides certification for a fixed finite pool, including
multiple-model routing, learned uncertainty scores, and cost-sensitive
variants~\cite{paclabels}. Its guarantees do not require an i.i.d.
population or a smooth score--error relation. Our problem instead concerns
sequential learning from paid feedback and cost regret against a population
oracle. Learning to defer also optimizes the allocation of predictions
between a model and an expert; consistent surrogate methods learn from
labeled training data and can allow an imperfect expert~\cite{defer}.
Their statistical target differs from a finite-horizon released-error
constraint with online acquisition costs.

Action-dependent label revelation is central to apple tasting and partial
monitoring~\cite{partial}. Online optimization with stochastic constraints
studies simultaneous objective regret and constraint
violation~\cite{stochastic}, but its full-feedback formulation supplies
constraint functions after each decision. Here that information must be
estimated from the labels actually purchased. Log-barrier mirror
descent~\cite{barrier} and adaptive squared-gradient scaling~\cite{adagrad}
are established ingredients. Our results concern their combination with
score approximation, selective feedback, and a common cost--error
comparator; they do not claim these ingredients or cost-sensitive routing
as new ideas. Section~\ref{sec:comparison} makes the comparison of
information and guarantees explicit.

\paragraph{Organization and prerequisites.}
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the information protocol, assumptions,
and comparator. Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} gives the complete algorithm,
and Sections~\ref{sec:upper-results}--\ref{sec:upper-proof} state and prove
its guarantees. Section~\ref{sec:lower} gives the lower bound and minimax
consequence, with the full construction in Appendix~\ref{app:cicat}.
The proofs use conditional expectation, convex duality, and elementary
concentration and information inequalities. The mirror-descent estimate
and concentration bound needed for the upper result are proved below.
We use $\widetilde O$ to suppress logarithmic factors; exact remainders
and their parameter dependence are displayed in the theorems.

\section{Stochastic model and population benchmark}
\label{sec:model}

\begin{assumption}[Fixed stochastic source]
\label{ass:iid}
The triples $(S_t,\widehat Y_t,Y_t)_{t=1}^T$ are i.i.d., with score
$S_t\in[0,1]$ and zero--one model loss
$L_t=\ind\{\widehat Y_t\ne Y_t\}$. The model and score mechanism are
fixed before the stream. No expert labels outside the specified feedback
protocol are available to the learner.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Costs and safe expert]
\label{ass:cost}
Calling the model costs a known constant $c_M\in[0,1]$ on every round.
An expert query costs one and returns the correct label $Y_t$.
An audit-and-correct action has the same expert charge and an additional
known cost $c_R\in[0,1]$ when the model label is wrong.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Global score regularity]
\label{ass:smooth}
The conditional error $m(s)=\Pp(L_t=1\mid S_t=s)$ admits a version on
$[0,1]$ with a known finite bound $L\ge0$ such that
\begin{equation}
 |m(s)-m(s')|\le L|s-s'|,\qquad s,s'\in[0,1].
 \label{eq:smooth}
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}
These assumptions describe a stationary labeling population and a reliable
expert. They impose neither calibration $m(s)=s$, monotonicity of $m$,
a score-density lower bound, nor conditions near an unknown optimal
threshold. Distribution shift, online retraining of the source, and
unrestricted changes to the score mechanism fall outside the theorem.
Observed drift, expert disagreement, or large local error differences can
challenge the assumptions; finite data alone do not certify a global
Lipschitz bound without additional structure.

\subsection{Information, losses, and costs}

At round $t$, the learner first fixes its state from the past, pays $c_M$,
and observes $(S_t,\widehat Y_t)$. It then chooses release, defer, or
audit using observable information and fresh randomness independent of
the current label. On release it outputs $\widehat Y_t$ and never
observes $Y_t$. Both expert actions reveal $Y_t$ and output that label;
an audited error is corrected. Only then is the state updated.
Let $\calH_{t-1}$ denote the observable history and state before the
current triple arrives. All initial-state and update kernels are fixed
independently of the unknown distribution.

For a general learner let $A_t\in\{\rel,\defr,\audit\}$ and
$I_t=\ind\{A_t\ne\rel\}$. Its actual released loss and total cost are
\begin{equation}
 R_t=(1-I_t)L_t,\qquad \calR_T=\sum_t R_t,\qquad
 C_T=c_MT+\sum_t\bigl(I_t+c_R\ind\{A_t=\audit\}L_t\bigr).
 \label{eq:ledger}
\end{equation}
The latent counterfactual loss $L_t$, the released loss $R_t$, and any
feedback estimate are different objects. In particular, a corrected label
has $R_t=0$. The proposed algorithm routes using the score and history;
the model prediction is used as the candidate output, not as an additional
routing feature.

\subsection{The comparator}

For a Borel release probability $a:[0,1]\to[0,1]$, define
\begin{equation}
 r_P(a)=\E[a(S)m(S)],\qquad
 c_P(a)=\E[1-a(S)],\qquad
 d_0(\eps)=\inf_{a:\,r_P(a)\le\eps}c_P(a),
 \label{eq:population}
\end{equation}
where the infimum is over such Borel functions. For $\eps\in(0,1]$,
the benchmark is
\begin{equation}
 C_T^\star(\eps)=T\{c_M+d_0(\eps)\}.
 \label{eq:population-benchmark}
\end{equation}
This policy may know the distribution, but cannot see the current true
label before acting. It uses the same score and pays the same model and
expert costs. Auditing is weakly dominated by direct deferral in this
model: both actions reveal and release $Y_t$, but audit corrections can
cost extra. Thus allowing audits does not lower this benchmark. Both
learner and comparator call the model on every round; a policy that can
skip this call is a different comparison problem.

We seek a simultaneous fixed-horizon event on which
\begin{equation}
 \calR_T\le\eps T+o(T),\qquad
 C_T\le C_T^\star(\eps)+o(T).
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
The benchmark cost is expected population cost, while $C_T$ and
$\calR_T$ are realized quantities. Probabilities in the upper bound
include both the i.i.d. data and the learner's coins.

\section{One-sided barrier saddle routing}
\label{sec:algorithm}

Partition $[0,1]$ into $K$ equal bins, assigning a boundary to the bin on
its right and including $1$ in the last bin. Write $b(s)$ for its bin.
For an integer horizon $T\ge2$, choose $K\ge1$ and
$\gamma\in[1/T,1)$. Maintain release probabilities
$x_t\in\mathcal X_\gamma=[0,1-\gamma]^K$ and a price
$\lambda_t\in[0,\Lambda]$, where
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda=\frac2\eps,\qquad
 \bar\eps=\min\{1,\eps+L/K\},\qquad
 \eta_x=\sqrt{\frac{K\log(1/\gamma)}{2(1+\Lambda^2)T}}.
 \label{eq:tuning}
\end{equation}
The relaxed internal budget $\bar\eps$ pays for score discretization;
it is accounted for explicitly in the final error bound. The reveal floor
$\gamma$ is an algorithm choice, not an assumption on the population.

At score $S_t$, let $z_t=x_{t,b_t}$, $b_t=b(S_t)$, and $q_t=1-z_t$.
Draw $D_t\sim\Ber(q_t)$ independently of the latent label conditional
on the past and current observation. If $D_t=1$, defer to the expert;
otherwise release the model label. Form the computable estimator
\begin{equation}
 W_t=\begin{cases}L_t/q_t,&D_t=1,\\0,&D_t=0,\end{cases}
 \qquad
 g_t=e_{b_t}(-1+\lambda_tW_t),\qquad
 h_t=z_tW_t-\bar\eps.
\label{eq:feedback}
\end{equation}
Here $e_j$ is the $j$th standard basis vector of $\mathbb R^K$.
Conditional on the past and score,
$\E[W_t\mid\calH_{t-1},S_t]=m(S_t)$. Setting $W_t=0$ after release
is computable and does not assert that the hidden error is zero.

The population saddle objective on the bins is
\begin{equation}
 d(x)+\lambda(r(x)-\bar\eps),\qquad
 d(x)=\E[1-x_{b(S)}],\quad r(x)=\E[x_{b(S)}m(S)].
 \label{eq:binned-functionals}
\end{equation}
The vectors in \eqref{eq:feedback} estimate its primal and dual gradients.
For the primal update use the one-sided log barrier and its Bregman divergence,
\begin{equation}
 \Psi(x)=-\sum_{j=1}^K\log(1-x_j),\qquad
 D_\Psi(u,v)=\Psi(u)-\Psi(v)-\langle\nabla\Psi(v),u-v\rangle.
 \label{eq:bregman}
\end{equation}
One mirror-descent step is
\begin{equation}
 x_{t+1}=\argmin_{x\in\mathcal X_\gamma}
       \{\eta_x\langle g_t,x\rangle+D_\Psi(x,x_t)\}.
 \label{eq:mirror}
\end{equation}
It changes only coordinate $b_t$. With
$\clip_{[a,b]}(v)=\min\{b,\max\{a,v\}\}$, its exact solution is
\begin{equation}
 v_t=q_t^{-1}-\eta_xg_{t,b_t},\qquad
 x_{t+1,b_t}=1-\frac1{\clip_{[1,1/\gamma]}(v_t)}.
 \label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}
Indeed the derivative of the scalar objective is $1/(1-x)-v_t$.
This formula handles boundary and large-step cases without an optimization
solver. The dual update is projected ascent with adaptive scaling:
\begin{equation}
 B_t=\sum_{u=1}^t h_u^2,\qquad
 \eta_{\lambda,t}=\frac\Lambda{\sqrt{1+B_t}},\qquad
 \lambda_{t+1}=\clip_{[0,\Lambda]}
            (\lambda_t+\eta_{\lambda,t}h_t).
 \label{eq:dual}
\end{equation}

\begin{algorithm}[tbp]
\caption{OBSR: one-sided barrier saddle routing}
\label{alg:obsr}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $T,\eps,L,K,\gamma$; parameters from \eqref{eq:tuning}.
\State Initialize $x_1=0\in\mathbb R^K$, $\lambda_1=0$, $B_0=0$.
\For{$t=1,\ldots,T$}
 \State Pay $c_M$ and observe $(S_t,\widehat Y_t)$; set $b_t=b(S_t)$.
 \State Set $z_t=x_{t,b_t}$ and $q_t=1-z_t$; draw $D_t\sim\Ber(q_t)$.
 \If{$D_t=1$}
  \State Pay one, observe $Y_t$, output $Y_t$, set $W_t=\ind\{\widehat Y_t\ne Y_t\}/q_t$.
 \Else
  \State Output $\widehat Y_t$, observe no true label, and set $W_t=0$.
 \EndIf
 \State Form $g_{t,b_t}=-1+\lambda_tW_t$ and $h_t=z_tW_t-\bar\eps$.
 \State Update coordinate $b_t$ by \eqref{eq:scalar}; leave all other coordinates unchanged.
 \State Set $B_t=B_{t-1}+h_t^2$ and update $\lambda_{t+1}$ by \eqref{eq:dual}.
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

OBSR uses no separate audit action: every acquired label is an expert
deferral, so $C_T=c_MT+\sum_tD_t$ and $R_t=(1-D_t)L_t$.
Deferral supplies the counterfactual model loss needed for learning while
ensuring a correct output on that round. After $O(K)$ initialization, the routing
updates take $O(1)$ arithmetic per round and $O(K)$ memory, excluding
model and expert calls. No score monotonicity is imposed on $x_t$.

\section{Joint guarantees and parameter choice}
\label{sec:upper-results}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-horizon OBSR guarantee]
\label{thm:upper}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:iid}--\ref{ass:smooth}, let $T\ge2$ be an
integer, $\eps\in(0,1]$, $\delta\in(0,1)$, $K\ge1$ an integer, and
$1/T\le\gamma<1$. Define
\begin{align}
 \ell_T&=\log(16KT/\delta),\notag\\
 H_T&=\sqrt{KT\ell_T}+\sqrt{T\ell_T/\gamma}+\ell_T/\gamma,\notag\\
 G_T(\eps)&=100(1+\eps^{-1})^2H_T.
 \label{eq:remainder}
\end{align}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:obsr} satisfies, simultaneously with probability at
least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 \calR_T\le\eps T+\frac{LT}{K}+G_T(\eps),\qquad
 C_T\le C_T^\star(\eps)+\gamma T+G_T(\eps).
 \label{eq:upper}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Balanced tuning]
\label{cor:balanced}
Take $K=\lceil T^{1/3}\rceil$ and $\gamma=T^{-1/3}$. On the event
in Theorem~\ref{thm:upper},
\begin{align}
 \calR_T-\eps T&\le LT^{2/3}+\overline G_T,\notag\\
 C_T-C_T^\star(\eps)&\le T^{2/3}+\overline G_T,\notag\\
 \overline G_T
 &=100(1+\eps^{-1})^2
 \bigl[(1+\sqrt2)T^{2/3}\sqrt{\ell_T}+T^{1/3}\ell_T\bigr].
 \label{eq:balanced}
\end{align}
For fixed $\eps,L$ and fixed or polynomially small $\delta$, both
remainders are $O_{\eps,L}(T^{2/3}\sqrt{\log(T/\delta)})=o(T)$.
The average released error is consequently at most
$\eps+\widetilde O(T^{-1/3})$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Use $K\le2T^{1/3}$, $LT/K\le LT^{2/3}$, and substitute into
\eqref{eq:remainder}. The $T^{1/3}\ell_T$ term is asymptotically
smaller than $T^{2/3}\sqrt{\ell_T}$ for the stated confidence levels.
\end{proof}

The three terms in $H_T$ represent primal learning over $K$ bins,
importance-weighted feedback variance, and its maximal magnitude.
Discretization contributes $LT/K$ to error; the reveal floor contributes
$\gamma T$ to cost. This makes the $T^{2/3}$ balance explicit.

\subsection{Nominal PAC control by tightening the target}

The bound in \eqref{eq:upper} targets $\eps$ with finite-sample slack.
The following consequence explains when one can certify the requested
nominal error while retaining the comparator at that requested target.

\begin{corollary}[A sufficient condition for nominal error control]
\label{cor:tightening}
Fix a requested target $\eps\in(0,1]$ and the other parameters of
Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}. Let
\begin{equation}
 \widehat G_T=100(1+2/\eps)^2H_T,\qquad
 b_T=\frac{L}{K}+\frac{\widehat G_T}{T}.
 \label{eq:tightening}
\end{equation}
If $b_T\le\eps/2$, run OBSR with internal target $e=\eps-b_T$,
recomputing \eqref{eq:tuning} using $e$. Then, simultaneously with
probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 \calR_T\le\eps T,\qquad
 C_T\le C_T^\star(\eps)+\gamma T+\widehat G_T+\frac{b_TT}{\eps}.
 \label{eq:nominal}
\end{equation}
Under balanced tuning and fixed $\eps,L$, the condition eventually
holds for fixed or polynomially small $\delta$, and the excess cost
in \eqref{eq:nominal} is $\widetilde O(T^{2/3})$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since $e\ge\eps/2$, $G_T(e)\le\widehat G_T$. Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}
at target $e$ gives $\calR_T\le eT+LT/K+G_T(e)\le\eps T$.
For any policy feasible at $\eps$, the scaled release probability
$a_e=(e/\eps)a$ is feasible at $e$ and costs at most
$c_P(a)+b_T/\eps$. Taking an infimizing sequence yields
$C_T^\star(e)\le C_T^\star(\eps)+b_TT/\eps$.
Apply the cost bound at $e$ and combine these inequalities.
\end{proof}
This is a sufficient finite-sample condition, not a promise of useful
savings at practical horizons. The displayed constants are conservative.
When the condition fails, always using the expert still ensures zero
released error, but need not have sublinear cost regret.

The comparison is between mathematical formulations, not measured

\section{Proof of the OBSR guarantee}
\label{sec:upper-proof}

For the analysis, let $\calF_t$ contain all triples and learner coins
through round $t$, including labels that were never revealed, and set
$\E_{t-1}=\E[\,\cdot\mid\calF_{t-1}]$. The observable history
satisfies $\calH_t\subseteq\calF_t$; the learner still uses only
$\calH_{t-1}$ and the permitted current observation. The state
$(x_t,\lambda_t)$ is $\calF_{t-1}$-measurable, and i.i.d. arrivals
and fresh coins give the conditional means below under this enlarged
filtration. In particular, $R_t-\E_{t-1}R_t$ is now an adapted
martingale difference although an unrevealed $R_t$ is not observable.
Write $\mathcal X_\gamma=[0,1-\gamma]^K$ and let
\begin{equation}
 d_\gamma^\star=\min_{x\in\mathcal X_\gamma:\ r(x)\le\bar\eps}d(x).
 \label{eq:binned-lp}
\end{equation}
An optimum exists by compactness and the safe policy $x=0$.

\begin{lemma}[Discretization and the safe-action multiplier]
\label{lem:coarsening}
We have $d_\gamma^\star\le d_0(\eps)+\gamma$. There is an optimal
multiplier $0\le\lambda^\star\le1/\bar\eps\le1/\eps$ such that
\begin{equation}
 d(x)-d_\gamma^\star+\lambda^\star(r(x)-\bar\eps)\ge0
 \quad\text{for every }x\in\mathcal X_\gamma.
 \label{eq:exact-penalty}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For any feasible Borel comparator $a$, define
$u_j=\E[a(S)\mid S\text{ in bin }j]$, setting it to zero on null bins,
and $\mu_j=\E[m(S)\mid S\text{ in bin }j]$ on positive-mass bins.
Conditional averaging preserves release mass. In each such bin,
\[
 u_j\mu_j-\E[a(S)m(S)\mid j]
 =\E[a(S)(\mu_j-m(S))\mid j]\le L/K.
\]
Summing with bin probabilities gives $r(u)\le\eps+L/K$; also
$r(u)\le1$, so $r(u)\le\bar\eps$. The vector
$u^\gamma=(1-\gamma)u$ lies in $\mathcal X_\gamma$, remains feasible,
and has $d(u^\gamma)\le c_P(a)+\gamma$. An infimizing sequence gives
the first conclusion without assuming a Borel minimizer exists.

Finite-dimensional LP duality yields an optimal $\lambda^\star\ge0$
for \eqref{eq:binned-lp}; strict feasibility follows from a sufficiently
small positive vector. Its dual identity gives \eqref{eq:exact-penalty}.
Evaluating the dual objective at $x=0$ gives
$0\le d_\gamma^\star\le1-\lambda^\star\bar\eps$, hence the bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Pathwise primal and dual regret]
\label{lem:regret}
If $K\log(1/\gamma)\le T/4$, then, for every $x\in\mathcal X_\gamma$,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_t\langle g_t,x_t-x\rangle
 \le A_x:=2\sqrt{2(1+\Lambda^2)KT\log(1/\gamma)}.
 \label{eq:primal-regret}
\end{equation}
For every $\lambda\in[0,\Lambda]$, without this restriction,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_t h_t(\lambda-\lambda_t)
 \le A_\lambda:=\frac32\Lambda\sqrt{1+\sum_t h_t^2}.
 \label{eq:dual-regret}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The key barrier cancellation is
\begin{equation}
 q_tg_{t,b_t}=-q_t+\lambda_tD_tL_t,\qquad
 q_t^2g_{t,b_t}^2\le2(1+\Lambda^2).
 \label{eq:cancellation}
\end{equation}
For the active coordinate, put $q=1-x_{t,b_t}$,
$\alpha=\eta_xqg_{t,b_t}$ and
$v=(x_{t+1,b_t}-x_{t,b_t})/q$. The assumed condition ensures
$|\alpha|\le1/2$. Mirror-descent optimality and the three-point
Bregman identity imply
\begin{align*}
 \eta_x\langle g_t,x_t-x\rangle
 &\le D_\Psi(x,x_t)-D_\Psi(x,x_{t+1})
       +\eta_x\langle g_t,x_t-x_{t+1}\rangle
       -D_\Psi(x_{t+1},x_t)\\
 &\le D_\Psi(x,x_t)-D_\Psi(x,x_{t+1})
       +\sup_{v<1}\{-\alpha v+\log(1-v)+v\}\\
 &=D_\Psi(x,x_t)-D_\Psi(x,x_{t+1})
       -\log(1-\alpha)-\alpha\\
 &\le D_\Psi(x,x_t)-D_\Psi(x,x_{t+1})+\alpha^2.
\end{align*}
The last scalar inequality holds for $|\alpha|\le1/2$.
Since $D_\Psi(x,0)=\sum_j[-\log(1-x_j)-x_j]\le K\log(1/\gamma)$,
telescoping and \eqref{eq:cancellation} give
\[
 \sum_t\langle g_t,x_t-x\rangle
 \le K\log(1/\gamma)/\eta_x+2\eta_x(1+\Lambda^2)T=A_x.
\]
For the dual step, nonexpansiveness of projection gives
\[
 h_t(\lambda-\lambda_t)
 \le\frac{(\lambda-\lambda_t)^2-(\lambda-\lambda_{t+1})^2}
              {2\eta_{\lambda,t}}
       +\frac{\eta_{\lambda,t}h_t^2}{2}.
\]
The step sizes decrease. Summation by parts bounds the first sum by
$\Lambda\sqrt{1+B_T}/2$; the second is at most
$\Lambda(\sqrt{1+B_T}-1)$, using
\[
 \frac{B_t-B_{t-1}}{\sqrt{1+B_t}}
 \le2(\sqrt{1+B_t}-\sqrt{1+B_{t-1}}).
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:dual-regret}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Martingale concentration]
\label{lem:bernstein}
If $(\xi_t)$ are martingale differences with $|\xi_t|\le b$ and
$\sum_t\E_{t-1}\xi_t^2\le V$ for deterministic $V$, then
\begin{equation}
 \Pp\left\{\sum_t\xi_t>\sqrt{2Vs}+bs/3\right\}\le e^{-s},
 \qquad s>0.
 \label{eq:bernstein}
\end{equation}
The same bound applies to the negative sum.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $0<\theta<3/b$, expanding the conditional exponential gives
\[
 \E_{t-1}e^{\theta\xi_t}
 \le\exp\left\{\frac{\theta^2\E_{t-1}\xi_t^2}{2(1-\theta b/3)}\right\}.
\]
Iteration bounds the moment-generating function of the sum by
$\exp\{V\theta^2/[2(1-\theta b/3)]\}$.
For $V>0$, exponential Markov with
$\theta=\sqrt{2s/V}/(1+(b/3)\sqrt{2s/V})$ gives \eqref{eq:bernstein}.
If $V=0$, all differences vanish almost surely.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A coupled population saddle bound]
\label{lem:saddle}
Suppose $K\log(1/\gamma)\le T/4$ and let $x^\star$ attain
\eqref{eq:binned-lp}. Write
\[
 U_T=\sum_t[d(x_t)-d_\gamma^\star],\qquad
 V_T=\sum_t[r(x_t)-\bar\eps].
\]
On a common event of probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 U_T+\lambda V_T\le25\eps^{-1}H_T\quad(\lambda=0,\Lambda),
 \qquad U_T\le25\eps^{-1}H_T,\quad (V_T)_+\le25H_T.
 \label{eq:saddle}
\end{equation}
The event can also include
\begin{equation}
 \sum_tD_t\le\sum_td(x_t)+J_T,\qquad
 \calR_T\le\sum_tr(x_t)+J_T,\qquad
 J_T=\sqrt{2T\ell_T}+\ell_T/3.
 \label{eq:realized}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adding \eqref{eq:primal-regret} and \eqref{eq:dual-regret} yields
\begin{equation}
 \sum_t[-z_t+x^\star_{b_t}+\lambda(z_tW_t-\bar\eps)]
 \le A_x+A_\lambda+N_T,\qquad
 N_T=\sum_t\lambda_t(x^\star_{b_t}W_t-\bar\eps).
 \label{eq:pathwise-saddle}
\end{equation}
Importance weighting and i.i.d. sampling give
$\E_{t-1}z_tW_t=r(x_t)$ and
$\E_{t-1}x^\star_{b_t}W_t=r(x^\star)\le\bar\eps$.
Define
\[
 M_0=\sum_t[x^\star_{b_t}-z_t-\E_{t-1}(x^\star_{b_t}-z_t)],
 \qquad M_r=\sum_t[z_tW_t-r(x_t)].
\]
The left side of \eqref{eq:pathwise-saddle} is
$U_T+\lambda V_T+M_0+\lambda M_r$.
The adaptive multiplier must stay inside $N_T$: its nonpositive
conditional drift follows from past-measurability of $\lambda_t$ and
feasibility of $x^\star$.

The moment calculations use $0\le W_t\le1/\gamma$ and
$\E[W_t^k\mid\calF_{t-1},S_t]\le q_t^{1-k}$ for $k\ge1$.
They give
\begin{align*}
 |h_t|&\le1/\gamma,&
 \E_{t-1}h_t^2&\le4/\gamma,&
 \E_{t-1}h_t^4&\le4/\gamma^3,\\
 |z_tW_t-r(x_t)|&\le1/\gamma,&
 \operatorname{Var}_{t-1}(z_tW_t)&\le1/\gamma.
\end{align*}
For $X_t=\lambda_t(x^\star_{b_t}W_t-\bar\eps)$,
$\E_{t-1}X_t\le0$, $\E_{t-1}X_t^2\le4\Lambda^2/\gamma$,
and $|X_t-\E_{t-1}X_t|\le2\Lambda/\gamma$.
Applying Lemma~\ref{lem:bernstein}, with $s=\ell_T$, gives the
following simultaneous bounds:
\begin{align}
 |M_0|&\le\sqrt{2T\ell_T}+2\ell_T/3,\notag\\
 |M_r|&\le\sqrt{2T\ell_T/\gamma}+\ell_T/(3\gamma),\notag\\
 N_T&\le\Lambda\sqrt{8T\ell_T/\gamma}
                      +2\Lambda\ell_T/(3\gamma),\notag\\
 \sum_th_t^2&\le4T/\gamma+\sqrt{8T\ell_T/\gamma^3}
                                 +\ell_T/(3\gamma^2).
 \label{eq:concentration-list}
\end{align}
For the last line, apply concentration to
$h_t^2-\E_{t-1}h_t^2$, whose range is bounded by $1/\gamma^2$
and conditional variance by $4/\gamma^3$. The two inequalities
in \eqref{eq:realized} follow because $D_t,R_t\in[0,1]$ have
conditional means $d(x_t),r(x_t)$. Counting the two tails for each
absolute value, there are eight tails in total. Their failure probability
is at most $8e^{-\ell_T}=\delta/(2KT)\le\delta$.

Put $P_T=\sqrt{KT\ell_T}$, $F_T=\sqrt{T\ell_T/\gamma}$, and
$Q_T=\ell_T/\gamma$, so $H_T=P_T+F_T+Q_T$.
Since $T\gamma\ge1$ and $\ell_T\ge1$,
\eqref{eq:concentration-list} implies
$\sqrt{1+\sum_th_t^2}\le5F_T+Q_T$.
Also $\log(1/\gamma)\le\ell_T$.
Substitution in \eqref{eq:pathwise-saddle}, using
$\Lambda=2/\eps$ and $\eps^{-1}\ge1$, bounds the coefficients
of $P_T,F_T,Q_T$ by $25\eps^{-1}$ for both $\lambda=0,\Lambda$.
This proves the first inequality in \eqref{eq:saddle}.

Finally, summing \eqref{eq:exact-penalty} at $x_t$ gives
$U_T+\lambda^\star V_T\ge0$. If $V_T>0$, the case $\lambda=\Lambda$
therefore yields
\[
 (\Lambda-\lambda^\star)V_T\le25\eps^{-1}H_T,
 \qquad \Lambda-\lambda^\star\ge1/\eps.
\]
This bounds $(V_T)_+$; the case $\lambda=0$ bounds $U_T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}]
In the stable regime $K\log(1/\gamma)\le T/4$, use the common
event in Lemma~\ref{lem:saddle}. Then
\[
 \calR_T\le\bar\eps T+25H_T+J_T
 \le\eps T+LT/K+G_T(\eps).
\]
Similarly, by Lemma~\ref{lem:coarsening},
\[
 C_T\le c_MT+Td_\gamma^\star+25\eps^{-1}H_T+J_T
 \le C_T^\star(\eps)+\gamma T+G_T(\eps).
\]
The coefficient $100(1+\eps^{-1})^2$ bounds all the displayed
remainders. Both sides include the same $c_MT$; neither the algorithm
nor an optimal comparator incurs audit correction charges.

If $K\log(1/\gamma)>T/4$, then
$\sqrt{KT\ell_T}>T/2$ and $G_T(\eps)\ge T$.
The deterministic bounds $\calR_T\le T$ and
$C_T-C_T^\star(\eps)\le T$ establish \eqref{eq:upper}.
This is a proof case; the scalar update \eqref{eq:scalar} remains
well-defined and the algorithm does not switch policies.
\end{proof}

\section{A complementary lower bound}
\label{sec:lower}

We now specialize the same model to binary labels, the constant predictor
$\widehat Y_t=0$, costs $c_M=c_R=0$, risk budget $\eps=1/4$, and
Lipschitz bound $L=1$. Thus $L_t=Y_t$, $C_T=\sum_tI_t$, and
$\calR_T=\sum_t(1-I_t)Y_t$. The lower bound allows every adaptive
release/defer/audit learner, not only binned policies or OBSR. Its initial
state and randomness cannot depend on the unknown instance. For an
instance $P_\theta$, abbreviate the same population benchmark by
\begin{equation}
 C^\star_{\theta,T}
 =T\inf_{a:\,\E_\theta[a(S)m_\theta(S)]\le1/4}
                \E_\theta[1-a(S)].
 \label{eq:benchmark}
\end{equation}
The infimum is again over all Borel $a:[0,1]\to[0,1]$. The following
construction is called CICAT (Camouflaged Independent-Cell Apple Tasting).

\begin{theorem}[CICAT cost--error lower bound]
\label{thm:main}
For every integer $T\ge8$, there is a finite family
$\{P_\theta:\theta\in\Theta_T\}$ satisfying the specialized model in Section~\ref{sec:lower}, with
$\Theta_T=\{-1,+1\}^{\lfloor T^{1/3}\rfloor}$, such that every admissible
randomized adaptive learner satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \max_{\theta\in\Theta_T}
 \left\{
  \E_\theta C_T-C^\star_{\theta,T}
  +2\left(\E_\theta\calR_T-\frac T4\right)
 \right\}
 \ge \frac{T^{2/3}}{2048}.
 \label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
Here $C^\star_{\theta,T}=C^\star_{P_\theta,T}(1/4)$, and $\E_\theta$ includes
the arrivals, labels, and learner randomization. The learner may know $T$,
the score marginal, and the entire family, but not the unknown $\theta$.
Every instance has an optimal comparator with risk exactly $1/4$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Cost lower bound under controlled error slack]
\label{cor:cost}
If a learner satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \E_\theta\calR_T\le\frac T4+\frac{T^{2/3}}{8192}
 \qquad\text{for every }\theta\in\Theta_T,
 \label{eq:slack}
\end{equation}
then, on at least one instance,
\begin{equation}
 \E_\theta C_T-C^\star_{\theta,T}\ge\frac{T^{2/3}}{4096}.
 \label{eq:cost-lower}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Choose an instance attaining the maximum in \eqref{eq:main} and subtract
twice the error excess. Equations \eqref{eq:main}--\eqref{eq:slack} give
$\E_\theta C_T-C^\star_{\theta,T}
\ge(1/2048-2/8192)T^{2/3}=T^{2/3}/4096$.
\end{proof}

The proof in Appendix~\ref{app:cicat} constructs the family, identifies
its binding optimal comparator, and bounds the information available to
any adaptive learner. The signed error excess in \eqref{eq:main} is
essential: the theorem alone is not a cost lower bound for a learner
allowed arbitrary error slack.

\subsection{A precise optimality consequence for nominal PAC labeling}

Target tightening permits a common comparison criterion for the upper and
lower results. The following definition fixes both the error requirement
and the comparator before comparing their rates.
Let $\mathcal P_1$ be the fixed class of all i.i.d. laws of
$(S,Y)\in[0,1]\times\{0,1\}$ with a globally one-Lipschitz version
of $\Pp(Y=1\mid S=s)$. Fix $\widehat Y\equiv0$, $c_M=c_R=0$,
unit expert cost, and $\eps=1/4$. For horizon $T$, define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal A_T^{\mathrm{PAC}}
 &=\left\{\mathcal A:
    \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_1}\Pp_P(\calR_T>T/4)\le T^{-2}\right\},
 \notag\\
 \mathfrak M_T
 &=\inf_{\mathcal A\in\mathcal A_T^{\mathrm{PAC}}}
   \sup_{P\in\mathcal P_1}
      \{\E_P C_T-C_{P,T}^\star(1/4)\}.
 \label{eq:minimax-definition}
\end{align}
The learners obey the information protocol and are fixed independently of
the unknown $P$; they may know the class and horizon. The class is
nonempty because always using the expert is admissible.

\begin{corollary}[Minimax exponent in a nominal-PAC class]
\label{cor:minimax}
There are universal constants $C<\infty$ and $T_0$ such that, for all
integers $T\ge T_0$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{T^{2/3}}{4096}\le\mathfrak M_T
       \le C T^{2/3}\sqrt{\log T}.
 \label{eq:minimax}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For any $\mathcal A\in\mathcal A_T^{\mathrm{PAC}}$, bounded loss gives
$\E_P\calR_T\le T/4+T\cdot T^{-2}=T/4+T^{-1}$, uniformly in $P$.
The finite CICAT family is contained in the fixed class $\mathcal P_1$.
For all sufficiently large $T$, $T^{-1}\le T^{2/3}/8192$, so
Corollary~\ref{cor:cost} gives the lower bound for each admissible
learner and hence for their infimum.

For the upper bound, take the tightened OBSR procedure in
Corollary~\ref{cor:tightening}, with $\eps=1/4$, $L=1$,
$\delta=T^{-2}$, $K=\lceil T^{1/3}\rceil$, and $\gamma=T^{-1/3}$.
Its finite-sample condition holds for all sufficiently large $T$,
uniformly over $\mathcal P_1$. It therefore belongs to
$\mathcal A_T^{\mathrm{PAC}}$ and has a uniform high-probability
cost gap $O(T^{2/3}\sqrt{\log T})$.
On the failure event, $C_T-C_{P,T}^\star(1/4)\le T$; taking
expectations adds at most $T^{-1}$. Taking the supremum over $P$
establishes the upper bound.
\end{proof}

This establishes the exponent for the specific criterion
\eqref{eq:minimax-definition}, up to a square-root logarithmic factor.
It does not characterize every error--cost tradeoff or imply the same
cost lower bound when failure probability is fixed, error slack is
larger, or the score regularity and comparator class change. In
particular, a fixed failure probability would contribute $O(T)$ in
the expectation conversion used above.

\section{Scope and empirical evaluation}
\label{sec:research}

\paragraph{Practical scope of the theory.}
OBSR learns a routing policy for a fixed predictor and score. Its bounds
apply to realized deployment costs and released errors, with expert
queries supplying all online label feedback. They do not provide a
guarantee for an adaptive pipeline that also retrains the predictor or
score on those labels. The nominal-PAC and minimax conclusions depend
on the explicit tightening condition and expectation conversion above.
The theorem constants may be too conservative to justify savings at
available dataset sizes. Sharper finite-sample analysis remains valuable
even if the asymptotic exponent is settled for this model.

\subsection{A fully specified calibration baseline}

For a population-stream comparison, consider a calibrated threshold
baseline inspired by PAC labeling~\cite{paclabels}. The following
independent-sample construction has its own deployment certificate;
it is not the published fixed-pool algorithm. Fix the predictor and score
before drawing $n\ge1$ calibration pairs $(S'_i,L'_i)$ i.i.d. from the
same law of $(S,L)$ as the deployment stream, independently of that stream.
For $j=0,\ldots,200$, let $a_j(s)=\ind\{s\le j/200\}$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \widehat r_j=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n a_j(S'_i)L'_i,\qquad
 u_n=\sqrt{\frac{\log(402/\delta)}{2n}},\qquad
 v_T=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/\delta)}{2T}}.
 \label{eq:calibration-baseline}
\end{equation}
Choose the largest $j$ with $\widehat r_j+u_n+v_T\le\eps$.
If none exists, use the expert-only policy $a\equiv0$, whose risk is
known to be zero. Freeze the selected policy and deploy it on an
independent stream of $T$ items. The largest feasible threshold minimizes
expert usage among the certified thresholds because their release sets
are nested, regardless of whether $m$ is monotone.

For completeness, Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over 201
thresholds give $r_P(a_j)\le\widehat r_j+u_n$ for every $j$ with
probability at least $1-\delta/2$. Conditional on calibration, a second
Hoeffding bound gives deployment average released error at most
$r_P(a_j)+v_T$ with probability at least $1-\delta/2$. Thus this
baseline satisfies $\Pp(\calR_T\le\eps T)\ge1-\delta$; the
expert-only fallback is safe deterministically. This certificate controls
error, but supplies no regret guarantee against all Borel score policies.
Its threshold restriction can exclude useful policies when $m$ is
nonmonotone. Charge $n(1+c_M)$ for calibration in addition to deployment
cost; report both quantities. An end-to-end comparison must also disclose
any labels used to train the fixed predictor or score.

\subsection{Proposed validation protocol}

No empirical OBSR result is claimed in this draft. A first comparison
should include balanced OBSR, its tightened variant when the sufficient
condition holds, the baseline in \eqref{eq:calibration-baseline}, and
always-expert labeling. The distribution-aware score-policy optimum is
an evaluation-only oracle. A separate batch study can compare the
published PAC-labeling procedure~\cite{paclabels} under its fixed-pool
protocol. Report the difference in pool access and use a common ledger
for all incurred model calls and expert queries; its finite-pool
certificate must be interpreted in that sampling model.

For a synthetic correctness study, draw i.i.d. scores
$S_t\sim\mathrm{Uniform}[0,1]$, then conditionally independently draw
$Y_t\mid S_t\sim\Ber(m(S_t))$, with $\widehat Y_t=0$.
Use $m(s)=0.05+0.4s$ and
$m(s)=0.05+0.4(2s-1)^2$, whose Lipschitz bounds are $0.4$ and $1.6$.
These are synthetic mechanisms with known oracle risks, not real model
evidence. A reproducible initial sweep would use
$T\in\{2^{10},2^{12},2^{14},2^{16},2^{18}\}$,
$\eps\in\{0.05,0.10,0.15\}$, $\delta=0.05$, and 500 trials
with seed $20260908+r$ for trial $r=0,\ldots,499$; methods share each
trial's stream and use separate routing random-number streams.
Use $n=\lceil0.2T\rceil$ for the independent calibration baseline and
the threshold grid and confidence allocation in
\eqref{eq:calibration-baseline}. This fixed calibration fraction is a
benchmark choice, not a sublinear-cost claim. Use $c_M\in\{0,0.05\}$,
$c_R=0$, and unit expert cost. OBSR uses the displayed balanced parameters
without oracle tuning. Record whether the target-tightening condition
holds at each horizon; if it fails, report that fact and the safe
expert-only fallback separately from the tightened method's cost bound.

Report actual released error, latent counterfactual model error used
only for evaluation, expert-query frequency, all cost components, and
gap to the same oracle comparator. Report violations both at nominal
$\eps$ and at the theorem's stated finite-horizon bound. For a real
benchmark, use independent held-out examples from genuine model
prediction tables. Repeated permutations of a fixed dataset do not
reproduce i.i.d. sampling conditional on that dataset; distinguish
such finite-dataset stress tests from the theorem's sampling model.
Distribution-shift studies must likewise be labeled outside the proven
stationary setting. A multi-source extension requires genuine distinct
models and a new theorem for its observation and cost structure.

\paragraph{Remaining research obligations.}
Independent proof review, a broader novelty audit against the public
literature, and an implementation with reproducible empirical validation
remain necessary before submission. Investigate
whether confidence-adaptive querying or sharper concentration improves
finite-sample cost while retaining nominal error control. Unknown
Lipschitz bounds, changing scores, multiple costly sources, and imperfect
experts require additional analysis; they are not covered by the results
above.

\section{Proof of the CICAT lower bound}
\label{app:cicat}
\subsection{The finite family}
\label{sec:family}

Fix $T\ge8$ and set
\begin{equation}
 M=\lfloor T^{1/3}\rfloor,\qquad
 \beta=\frac1{64(M+1)},\qquad
 \bar\theta=\frac1M\sum_{i=1}^M\theta_i,\qquad
 s_i=\frac12+\frac{i}{2(M+1)}.
 \label{eq:parameters}
\end{equation}
Here $\beta$ is a signal gap, not a failure probability. We have
$M\ge2$ and $\beta\le1/192$. The score marginal is the same for all
$\theta\in\{-1,+1\}^M$, and the conditional law is
$Y\mid S=s\sim\Ber(m_\theta(s))$, with
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Score & Probability & Conditional error $m_\theta(s)$\\
\midrule
$0$ (anchor) & $1/4$ & $1/8-\beta\bar\theta$\\[2pt]
$1/2$ (boundary) & $1/2$ & $1/2$\\[2pt]
$s_i$, $i=1,\ldots,M$ & $1/(4M)$ & $1/2+\theta_i\beta$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Between consecutive support points, extend $m_\theta$ by linear
interpolation, and keep it constant on $[s_M,1]$. Draw the $T$ pairs
independently from this law. This is a constructed lower-bound family,
not an empirical dataset.

\begin{lemma}[Admissibility and common marginals]
\label{lem:family}
Every $P_\theta$ satisfies \eqref{eq:smooth} with $L=1$. The score marginal, the
model output, and the marginal label law are identical across the family;
in particular, $\E_\theta Y=13/32$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The probabilities sum to one, and all conditional means lie in $(0,1)$.
The interpolation slope on $[0,1/2]$ is
$3/4+2\beta\bar\theta$, whose absolute value is less than one.
On $[1/2,s_1]$ its absolute value is $2\beta(M+1)=1/32$;
between successive $s_i$ it is at most $4\beta(M+1)=1/16$;
after $s_M$ it is zero. Hence the continuous extension is globally
one-Lipschitz. Finally,
\[
 \E_\theta Y
 =\frac14\left(\frac18-\beta\bar\theta\right)
  +\frac1{4M}\sum_{i=1}^M\left(\frac12+\theta_i\beta\right)
  +\frac12\cdot\frac12
 =\frac{13}{32}.
\]
Since $Y$ is binary, its marginal law is therefore common as well.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Optimal comparator and the cost--error decomposition}

Let $n_-=\#\{i:\theta_i=-1\}=M(1-\bar\theta)/2$ and define
\begin{align}
 r_\theta
 &=\frac14\left(\frac18-\beta\bar\theta\right)
   +\frac{n_-}{4M}\left(\frac12-\beta\right)\notag\\
 &=\frac3{32}-\frac\beta8
   -\bar\theta\left(\frac1{16}+\frac\beta8\right),
 \qquad \alpha_\theta=1-4r_\theta.
 \label{eq:boundary-mix}
\end{align}
Define $a^\star_\theta$ on the support by
\begin{equation}
 a^\star_\theta(0)=1,\qquad
 a^\star_\theta(1/2)=\alpha_\theta,\qquad
 a^\star_\theta(s_i)=\ind\{\theta_i=-1\},
 \label{eq:comparator}
\end{equation}
and extend it as a Borel function, for example by setting it to zero off
the support.

\begin{lemma}[Optimality and excess objective]
\label{lem:comparator}
The policy $a^\star_\theta$ is optimal in \eqref{eq:benchmark}, has risk
exactly $1/4$, and satisfies
\begin{equation}
 C^\star_{\theta,T}
 =T\left(\frac5{16}-\frac\beta4(1+\bar\theta)\right).
 \label{eq:explicit-cost}
\end{equation}
For any learner, set
\begin{align}
 \Delta_\theta
 &=\E_\theta C_T-C^\star_{\theta,T}
   +2(\E_\theta\calR_T-T/4),\label{eq:priced-gap}\\
 W_i
 &=\sum_{t=1}^T\ind\{S_t=s_i\}
   \bigl(\ind\{\theta_i=-1\}I_t
        +\ind\{\theta_i=+1\}(1-I_t)\bigr).
 \label{eq:wrong-actions}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_\theta\ge2\beta\sum_{i=1}^M\E_\theta W_i.
 \label{eq:decomposition}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:boundary-mix} gives
$1/32-\beta/4\le r_\theta\le5/32$, so $0<r_\theta<1/4$
and $0<\alpha_\theta<1$. The anchor and negative cells contribute risk
$r_\theta$, and the boundary contributes $\alpha_\theta/4$.
Thus $r_{P_\theta}(a^\star_\theta)=1/4$. Its deferral probability is
$(M-n_-)/(4M)+(1-\alpha_\theta)/2$; simplifying yields
\eqref{eq:explicit-cost} once optimality is established.

For a policy $a$, introduce
\[
 J_\theta(a)=c_{P_\theta}(a)+2r_{P_\theta}(a)
 =\E_\theta[1+a(S)(2m_\theta(S)-1)].
\]
The policy \eqref{eq:comparator} minimizes the integrand at every support
point: it releases below error $1/2$, reveals above $1/2$, and may mix
at equality. For any feasible $a$,
\[
 c_{P_\theta}(a)
 =J_\theta(a)-2r_{P_\theta}(a)
 \ge J_\theta(a^\star_\theta)-\frac12
 =c_{P_\theta}(a^\star_\theta),
\]
proving optimality.

Because the action precedes the label and the stream is i.i.d.,
\[
 \E_\theta[I_t+2R_t\mid\calH_{t-1},S_t,A_t]
 =I_t+2(1-I_t)m_\theta(S_t).
\]
The comparator's expected total objective is
$TJ_\theta(a^\star_\theta)=C^\star_{\theta,T}+T/2$.
Hence $\Delta_\theta$ is the learner's excess expected objective over
this pointwise minimum. At cell $i$, revealing when $\theta_i=-1$
or releasing when $\theta_i=+1$ incurs excess $2\beta$.
The excess is zero at the boundary and nonnegative at the anchor.
Summing and discarding the anchor excess gives \eqref{eq:decomposition}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Indistinguishability and adaptive decisions}

For probability laws $P,Q$, write $\KL(P\|Q)$ for relative entropy
with natural logarithms, and $\TV(P,Q)=\sup_B|P(B)-Q(B)|$.
Fix a coordinate $i$ and all other signs. Denote the two resulting
instances by $\theta^-$ and $\theta^+$, according to the sign of
coordinate $i$.

\begin{lemma}[Information bound]
\label{lem:information}
For every fixed learner and every $t\le T$, the laws $\nu_t^-$ and
$\nu_t^+$ of $\calH_{t-1}$ under the paired instances satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \KL(\nu_t^-\|\nu_t^+)<\frac1{100},\qquad
 \TV(\nu_t^-,\nu_t^+)<\frac14.
 \label{eq:information}
\end{equation}
The same bounds hold for the complete observable interaction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $p,q\in(0,1)$, $\log x\le x-1$ implies
\begin{equation}
 \KL(\Ber(p)\|\Ber(q))
 \le\frac{(p-q)^2}{q(1-q)}.
 \label{eq:bernoulli-kl}
\end{equation}
The paired laws differ only at $s_i$ and the anchor. At $s_i$ their
means differ by $2\beta$, and $q(1-q)=1/4-\beta^2>4/17$,
so the conditional divergence is at most $17\beta^2$.
At the anchor the difference is $2\beta/M$, and
\[
 q\in[23/192,25/192],\qquad
 q(1-q)\ge\frac{23\cdot169}{192^2}>\frac1{10}.
\]
Its conditional divergence is therefore at most $40\beta^2/M^2$.

Consider the full latent sample $(S_t,Y_t)_{t=1}^T$, including labels
never revealed to the learner, and denote its law by $Q_\theta$.
The common score marginal and product additivity give
\begin{align}
 \KL(Q_{\theta^-}\|Q_{\theta^+})
 &\le T\left(\frac{17\beta^2}{4M}
                    +\frac{10\beta^2}{M^2}\right)\notag\\
 &\le T\beta^2\left(\frac{17}{4M}+\frac{40}{M^2}\right)\notag\\
 &<\frac{M+1}{4096}\left(\frac{17}{4M}+\frac{40}{M^2}\right)
 \le\frac{291}{32768}<\frac1{100}.
 \label{eq:latent-kl}
\end{align}
Here $T<(M+1)^3$, and, for $M\ge2$,
\[
 (M+1)\left(\frac{17}{4M}+\frac{40}{M^2}\right)
 =\frac{17}{4}+\frac{177}{4M}+\frac{40}{M^2}
 \le\frac{291}{8}.
\]
The learner's random seed is independent of this sample and has the same
law under both instances. Every observable history, and the entire
interaction, is a common randomized transformation of the latent sample.
Data processing transfers \eqref{eq:latent-kl} to these laws.
Pinsker's inequality then gives
$\TV\le\sqrt{\KL/2}<1/\sqrt{200}<1/4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Paired action errors]
\label{lem:testing}
For every paired $\theta^-,\theta^+$,
\begin{equation}
 \E_{\theta^-}\sum_{t=1}^T\ind\{S_t=s_i\}I_t
 +\E_{\theta^+}\sum_{t=1}^T\ind\{S_t=s_i\}(1-I_t)
 \ge\frac{3T}{16M}.
 \label{eq:paired-errors}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $k_{t,i}(h)\in[0,1]$ be the learner's probability of revealing at
score $s_i$ given history $h$, integrating over its fresh randomness.
This is a common kernel under the two instances. Put
$q_t^\pm=\int k_{t,i}\,d\nu_t^\pm$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:information},
\[
 |q_t^--q_t^+|\le\TV(\nu_t^-,\nu_t^+)<\frac14,
 \qquad q_t^-+1-q_t^+\ge\frac34.
\]
Under each instance, $S_t$ is independent of the pre-score history
and $\Pp(S_t=s_i)=1/(4M)$. Thus the left side of
\eqref{eq:paired-errors} equals
\[
 \frac1{4M}\sum_{t=1}^T(q_t^-+1-q_t^+)
 \ge\frac{3T}{16M}.
\]
Comparing histories \emph{before} the current score arrives is essential:
no conditioning of a joint total-variation bound on a rare score event
is used.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Completion of the lower-bound proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Lemma~\ref{lem:family} establishes admissibility, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:comparator} supplies the binding optimal comparator.
For each coordinate $i$, partition $\Theta_T$ into the $2^{M-1}$
pairs differing only at $i$. Lemma~\ref{lem:testing} gives
\[
 \frac1{2^M}\sum_{\theta\in\Theta_T}\E_\theta W_i
 \ge\frac{2^{M-1}}{2^M}\frac{3T}{16M}
 =\frac{3T}{32M}.
\]
Using \eqref{eq:decomposition} and summing over $i$,
\[
 \max_\theta\Delta_\theta
 \ge\frac1{2^M}\sum_\theta\Delta_\theta
 \ge2\beta\frac{3T}{32}
 =\frac{3T}{1024(M+1)}.
\]
Since $T^{1/3}\ge2$ and $M+1\le T^{1/3}+1\le(3/2)T^{1/3}$,
the last quantity is at least $T^{2/3}/512$, which is stronger than
the stated $T^{2/3}/2048$. This proves \eqref{eq:main}.
\end{proof}
